Internal Memo: Client Services Perspective on the Four-Day Pilot

Client Services was not part of the pilot but supports the Engineering
teams that were, and raised the following concerns and observations during
the pilot debrief.

Coverage: three enterprise clients had support tickets that were opened
Thursday evening and not addressed by an engineer until the following
Monday, compared to a same-day-or-next-day response under the normal
five-day schedule. Two of these were flagged by the client as a
noticeable slowdown; account managers had to intervene with a status
update to keep clients satisfied. Client Services proposed that any wider
rollout stagger the day off across teams so at least partial engineering
coverage exists five days a week, rather than the whole department being
off the same day.

Sales impact: the sales team reported no client ever raised the pilot
publicly as a concern, and one prospective client reacted positively when
told about it during a hiring-culture conversation, though this is
anecdotal and Sales did not track it systematically.

Contract terms: two enterprise contracts include a "next business day"
response-time SLA that was technically at risk during the pilot, though
no formal SLA breach was recorded because the department temporarily
authorized on-call rotation to cover urgent Friday tickets. Client
Services recommends that any permanent policy explicitly fund and staff
a small Friday on-call rotation rather than relying on informal
volunteer coverage, which is what happened during the pilot.

Overall, Client Services's position is that the model is workable but
NOT free -- it has a real, quantifiable coverage cost that needs a
deliberate staffing answer, not just goodwill from engineers picking up
Friday tickets voluntarily.
