Internal Memo: Finance Review of the Four-Day Week Proposal

Finance modeled three scenarios for extending the four-day, full-pay
model company-wide (currently ~310 employees).

Direct payroll cost: since the pilot kept full pay for reduced hours,
there is no direct payroll savings, and Finance's base case assumes no
headcount reduction, so payroll cost is roughly flat. Finance flagged a
risk that if the four-day week requires backfilling coverage (e.g. the
Friday on-call rotation Client Services requested), that adds an
estimated $180K-$260K/year in on-call premiums and overtime across the
company if rolled out broadly, based on current on-call pay rates.

Recruiting and retention: Finance's model treats reduced attrition as the
main financial upside, since replacing a mid-level engineer costs an
estimated 6-9 months of that role's fully loaded salary in recruiting,
onboarding, and lost productivity. Even a modest reduction in voluntary
attrition (the pilot saw zero departures in-window, though Finance
considers the sample too small to rely on) could offset a meaningful share
of the on-call staffing cost above.

Office and facilities: a company-wide four-day week with a staggered day
off (as Client Services proposed) would not reduce facilities costs, since
the office would still be in use five days a week. A company-wide single
day off (e.g. all of Engineering off Fridays) could allow reduced Friday
facilities costs (cleaning, front desk staffing), estimated at a modest
$15K-$25K/year in savings -- small relative to the on-call cost above.

Finance's overall position: the proposal is not cost-neutral once realistic
coverage staffing is included, and the financial case depends heavily on
whether the retention and productivity gains seen in the small pilot hold
up at full-company scale, which Finance considers the single biggest
unresolved question before a company-wide commitment.
