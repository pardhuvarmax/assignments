Internal Memo: Q1 Four-Day Week Pilot -- Engineering Department Results

Acme Robotics ran a 12-week pilot of a four-day, 32-hour work week (Monday
through Thursday, full pay) in the Engineering department, covering 45
engineers across three teams. The pilot compared the 12 weeks against the
prior 12-week baseline on the same teams.

Output metrics: story points completed per sprint were roughly flat,
down about 3% on average across the three teams, but within the normal
sprint-to-sprint variance seen in the two quarters before the pilot. One
team (Platform) saw a 6% increase, which the team lead attributed to
fewer context-switching interruptions since meetings were compressed into
four days. Code review turnaround time improved slightly, from a median of
14 hours to 11 hours, which engineering leadership believes is because
engineers had less unscheduled time to let reviews sit.

Sick-day usage dropped by about 18% compared to the same 12 weeks the
prior year. Voluntary attrition in the pilot teams was zero during the
pilot window, compared to 2 departures in the same period the year before,
though the sample is too small to draw a strong conclusion from that alone.

Meeting load: the teams self-reported cutting recurring meetings by
roughly 25%, mostly by canceling weekly status meetings in favor of an
async written update, and by consolidating two standing planning meetings
into one.

Open concerns from the pilot: two engineers reported feeling pressure to
"cram" a five-day workload into four days rather than actually reducing
scope, and said they worked longer hours on the four days to compensate.
Engineering leadership flagged this as a management-practice issue to
address before any wider rollout, not a fundamental flaw in the four-day
model itself -- teams that proactively cut lower-priority work saw better
results than teams that just compressed their existing five-day plan.
